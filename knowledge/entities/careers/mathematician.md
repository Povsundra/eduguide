# Career Entity: Mathematician

## 1. Basic Information
- **Career name:** Mathematician
- **Khmer name:** គណិតវិទូ (Kanetvithou)
- **Career category:** Science / Academia & Research
- **Industry:** Higher Education, Research Institutions, Finance/Banking, Government, Engineering & Technology
- **Career level:** Entry to Senior (Research Assistant/Junior Lecturer → Lecturer/Analyst → Senior Lecturer/Senior Analyst → Professor/Principal Researcher)
- **Alternative job titles:** Applied Mathematician, Mathematics Lecturer/Professor, Quantitative Analyst, Research Mathematician, Mathematics Researcher

## 2. Career Overview
A Mathematician studies and applies mathematical theories, models, and techniques to understand abstract concepts and solve practical problems across business, government, engineering, and science. The main purpose of the role is either to advance mathematical knowledge (theoretical/pure mathematics) or to apply mathematical methods to real-world problems (applied mathematics), such as data analysis, modeling, and forecasting. In Cambodia, most mathematicians work within higher education as lecturers/researchers, since specialized "mathematician" roles outside academia are limited; applied mathematical skills are also valued in finance, engineering, and data-related fields. Within a university, a mathematician typically works within a Department of Mathematics or Science faculty, reporting to a Dean or Head of Department.

## 3. Responsibilities
- Conduct research in fundamental (pure) mathematics or apply mathematical techniques to science, engineering, business, or social science problems
- Develop mathematical models, formulate hypotheses, and construct proofs
- Perform computations and numerical analysis on data
- Teach mathematics courses and mentor students (common in Cambodia's academic-dominated job market)
- Disseminate research through reports, papers, and conference presentations
- Collaborate with professionals in related fields (engineering, computer science, economics) to solve applied problems
- Maintain up-to-date knowledge of the field through journals, conferences, and professional networks
- Use computational/statistical software and coding to build and test models

## 4. Required Skills

### Technical Skills
- Strong foundation in mathematics (algebra, calculus, statistics, number theory, etc.)
- Mathematical and statistical modeling
- Numerical analysis and computation
- Proficiency with analytical/scientific software and basic programming
- Research methodology and academic writing
- (For applied roles) Knowledge of the target domain — e.g., finance, engineering, data science

### Soft Skills
- Analytical and critical thinking
- Problem-solving
- Attention to detail
- Written and verbal communication (teaching, publishing, presenting)
- Ability to work independently and collaboratively
- Patience and persistence (research and teaching both require sustained effort)

## 5. Work Environment
- **Common workplaces:** Universities and higher education institutions, research centers, government agencies, banks/financial institutions, engineering and technology companies
- **Office work:** Predominant — teaching preparation, research, and writing
- **Laboratory/field work:** Not typical, though some applied roles involve data collection or collaborative fieldwork with other disciplines
- **Remote work:** Increasingly possible for research and some teaching/consulting work
- **Academic conferences/travel:** Common for research dissemination and networking

## 7. Job Outlook
- In Cambodia, most mathematics-focused careers exist within the growing higher education sector, where universities such as the Institute of Technology of Cambodia, the University of Cambodia, Build Bright University, and newer institutions like De Montfort University Cambodia continue to expand faculties in science, engineering, and technology.
- Cambodia's higher education sector is expanding rapidly, with new institutions (e.g., DMUC, the first UK-licensed university in Cambodia) opening science- and technology-related programs and hiring academic staff.
- Outside academia, applied mathematical skills are increasingly valued in Cambodia's growing finance, data analytics, and engineering sectors, though dedicated "mathematician" job titles are rare — such skills are typically absorbed into roles like data analyst, quantitative analyst, or engineer.
- Globally, demand for mathematician and statistician roles has been projected to grow significantly faster than average, reflecting rising need for quantitative and analytical skills across industries — a trend likely to extend into Cambodia's developing data-driven sectors.

## 9. Cambodia Career Context
- **Common employers:** Universities (Institute of Technology of Cambodia, University of Cambodia, Build Bright University, De Montfort University Cambodia, CamTech University, Royal University of Phnom Penh), research institutions, banks (for quantitative/applied roles)
- **Industries hiring:** Higher education (dominant), banking/finance (applied mathematics/data roles), engineering and technology
- **Government demand:** Limited direct demand for "mathematician" titles, though government-linked research and statistics bodies may employ quantitative specialists
- **Private sector demand:** Growing indirectly — banks, fintech, and engineering firms increasingly value strong mathematical/quantitative backgrounds, often under titles like data analyst or quantitative analyst rather than "mathematician"
- **International organization demand:** Limited but present — international research collaborations and donor-funded higher-education partnerships (e.g., ARES-supported projects at ITC) sometimes involve applied mathematics/data expertise

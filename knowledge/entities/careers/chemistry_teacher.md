# Career Entity: Chemistry Teacher

## 1. Basic Information
- **Career name:** Chemistry Teacher
- **Khmer name:** គ្រូបង្រៀនគីមីវិទ្យា (Kru Bangrien Kimividya)
- **Career category:** Education
- **Industry:** Public Education, Private/International Education
- **Career level:** Entry to Senior (Junior Teacher → Teacher → Senior/Lead Teacher → Head of Department)
- **Alternative job titles:** Secondary Science Teacher (Chemistry), Chemistry & Biology Teacher, IGCSE/A-Level Chemistry Teacher, Science Teacher

## 2. Career Overview
A Chemistry Teacher plans and delivers lessons on chemical elements, compounds, reactions, and scientific principles to secondary school students, while supervising laboratory experiments and assessing student progress. The main purpose of the role is to build students' scientific literacy, critical thinking, and lab safety awareness, preparing them for further study or STEM careers. In Cambodia, the role sits within both the public education system (under the Ministry of Education, Youth and Sport) and a growing private/international school sector. Within a school, a Chemistry Teacher typically works within a Science Department, reporting to a Head of Science/Department Head or School Principal.

## 3. Responsibilities
- Plan and deliver lessons on chemistry topics aligned with curriculum standards (e.g., national curriculum, IGCSE, A-Level)
- Design syllabi, schedules, and assessment plans
- Supervise laboratory experiments and enforce lab safety protocols
- Set and grade tests, assignments, and practical exams fairly
- Develop rapport with students and adapt teaching to different learning needs and aptitudes
- Monitor student behavior, progress, and discipline
- Attend parent-teacher meetings and student events
- Participate in professional development and continuous training (e.g., pedagogy, STEM integration, ICT skills)
- Occasionally escort students on field trips or extracurricular science activities

## 4. Required Skills

### Technical Skills
- Strong subject knowledge of chemistry (chemical processes, lab techniques)
- Instructional strategies and curriculum/lesson planning
- Laboratory safety and experiment supervision
- Assessment and grading methods
- ICT skills for digital teaching tools and learning platforms
- (For international schools) Familiarity with specific curricula such as IGCSE/A-Level or the American/British national curriculum

### Soft Skills
- Communication and ability to explain complex concepts clearly
- Patience and adaptability with students of varying abilities
- Classroom management and discipline
- Creativity in designing engaging lessons
- Collaboration with colleagues and department teams
- Mentoring and relationship-building with students

## 5. Work Environment
- **Common workplaces:** Public secondary schools, private schools, international schools, teacher training institutes
- **Office/classroom work:** Predominant — classroom teaching and lesson preparation
- **Laboratory work:** Regular — supervising practical chemistry experiments
- **Field work:** Occasional — science field trips or competitions
- **Remote work:** Rare, though some blended/online professional development exists

## 7. Job Outlook
- Cambodia's Ministry of Education, Youth and Sport (MoEYS) has prioritized strengthening STEM education in secondary schools, including through the Secondary Education Development Project and Upper Secondary Education Sector Development Programme, which support science teaching capacity nationwide.
- A national continuing professional development (CPD) system is expanding, with tens of thousands of teachers registered and new accredited courses covering science teaching, ICT skills, and classroom management.
- Cambodian officials and teachers have participated in international AI-STEM training programs (e.g., with India's ITEC program) to strengthen digital and pedagogical competencies for science educators.
- International and private schools in Phnom Penh (and increasingly other cities) are expanding, creating demand for qualified Chemistry/Science teachers, particularly those able to teach IGCSE/A-Level or other international curricula.
- The National Institute of Education (NIE) continues to be the main teacher-training institution, with efforts to raise teacher qualifications to postgraduate level.

## 9. Cambodia Career Context
- **Common employers:** Public secondary schools under MoEYS, private and international schools (e.g., in Phnom Penh), the National Institute of Education (for teacher trainers)
- **Industries hiring:** Public education, private K-12 education, international schooling sector
- **Government demand:** Ongoing — national STEM education initiatives and CPD programs indicate sustained government investment in science teaching capacity
- **Private sector demand:** Growing — expansion of international schools (offering IGCSE, A-Level, and other foreign curricula) is creating demand for qualified, often internationally credentialed, Chemistry teachers
- **International organization demand:** Present — development partners (e.g., ADB-supported programs) fund STEM and secondary education capacity-building projects involving science teacher training

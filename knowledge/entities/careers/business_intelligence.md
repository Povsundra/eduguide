## 2. Career Overview

A Business Intelligence (BI) Analyst gathers data from across an organization's systems and turns it into reports, dashboards, and key performance indicators (KPIs) that support management decision-making. The role centers on making existing operational and financial data understandable and actionable — through dashboards, regular reporting, and ad-hoc analysis — rather than on building new predictive models from scratch.

- **Main purpose:** Deliver clear, reliable, decision-ready reporting and dashboards that help managers and executives monitor performance and make informed choices.
- **Importance:** As organizations accumulate transactional, operational, and customer data, BI Analysts act as the bridge between raw data and business decisions, ensuring leadership has an accurate, up-to-date view of performance.
- **Typical role within an organization:** Sits within a data/analytics, finance, or IT department; works closely with business units (sales, operations, finance, risk) to understand their reporting needs and with IT/data engineering teams to access and structure the underlying data.

---

## 3. Responsibilities

- Gather requirements from business stakeholders to define what needs to be measured and reported
- Define and track key performance indicators (KPIs) for different business units
- Build and maintain dashboards and reports (e.g., in Power BI, Tableau, or similar tools)
- Extract, clean, and organize data from multiple source systems (databases, ERP, CRM, core banking systems)
- Conduct ad-hoc analysis to answer specific business questions
- Monitor data quality and flag inconsistencies or errors in reporting
- Present findings and insights to non-technical stakeholders and management
- Collaborate with IT/data engineering teams on data pipelines and data warehousing needs
- Document reporting logic, metric definitions, and data sources for consistency
- Recommend process or reporting improvements based on identified trends

**Typical decision-making responsibilities:** Choosing appropriate KPIs and visualizations for a given business question, deciding how to structure a dashboard for clarity, and determining when data quality issues are significant enough to flag before reporting results.

## 4. Required Skills

### Technical Skills
- Business intelligence tools (Power BI, Tableau, or similar)
- SQL and database querying
- Microsoft Excel (advanced: pivot tables, formulas, data modeling)
- Data warehousing and ETL concepts
- Basic statistics for interpreting trends and patterns
- Familiarity with ERP/ CRM or core banking systems (industry-dependent)
- Data visualization and dashboard design principles
- Python or R (increasingly useful, though not always required at entry level)

### Soft Skills
- Communication (translating data into insights for non-technical stakeholders)
- Business acumen (understanding what metrics matter and why)
- Problem solving
- Attention to detail
- Stakeholder management
- Critical thinking

## 7. Job Outlook

- **Current demand:** Present across banking, manufacturing, and multinational companies operating in Cambodia; roles range from entry-level reporting positions to senior BI developer roles at large multinational employers with a Phnom Penh presence (e.g., a Senior Business Intelligence Developer role posted by a major multinational consumer goods company in Phnom Penh).
- **Future demand:** Business intelligence work continues to be identified as a broadly in-demand field globally, spanning finance, retail, logistics, manufacturing, and government, and Cambodia's expanding banking and manufacturing sectors point toward continued local demand for reporting and analytics roles.
- **Emerging technologies:** Cloud-based BI platforms (e.g., Microsoft Fabric/Power BI ecosystem), AI-assisted dashboard generation and narrative summarization, self-service analytics tools that let business users build their own reports with BI Analyst oversight.
- **Industry trends:** The field is shifting from static, one-off reporting toward governed, embedded, and collaborative analytics, meaning BI Analysts increasingly need platform and data-governance awareness rather than only dashboard-building skills; AI is expected to automate routine dashboard drafting but not the judgment needed to define metrics and validate data quality.
- **Growth opportunities:** Strong entry point into the broader data/analytics field; banks, manufacturers, and multinational companies operating in Cambodia offer a realistic path from junior reporting roles into senior BI, data analytics, or data science roles.

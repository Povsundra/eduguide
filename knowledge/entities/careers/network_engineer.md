# Career Entity: Network Engineer

## 1. Basic Information

- **Career name:** Network Engineer
- **Khmer name:** វិស្វករបណ្តាញ (Visavakâr Bantoănh)
- **Career category:** Technology / IT Infrastructure
- **Industry:** Telecommunications, Banking & Finance, NGOs & Development, Government, Internet Service Providers (ISPs)
- **Career level:** Entry to Senior
- **Alternative job titles:** Network Administrator, IT Network Engineer, Network Support Engineer, IP Network Engineer, Systems/Network Engineer

---

## 2. Career Overview

A Network Engineer designs, builds, and maintains the communication infrastructure that connects computers, servers, and devices within and across organizations — including local area networks (LAN), wide area networks (WAN), wireless networks, and internet connectivity. The role ensures that data flows reliably, securely, and efficiently, and is responsible for diagnosing and resolving connectivity issues.

- **Main purpose:** Keep an organization's network infrastructure running reliably, securely, and at the performance level the business needs.
- **Importance:** Every digital service — banking transactions, internet access, internal business systems — depends on functioning network infrastructure; in Cambodia, internet service providers and telecom operators rely directly on network engineers to maintain fiber and IP backbone networks covering large parts of the country.
- **Typical role within an organization:** Sits within an IT or network operations team; in telecom/ISP companies, may specialize in core/backbone network operations, while in banks, NGOs, or general businesses the role often covers broader IT infrastructure support.

---

## 3. Responsibilities

- Design, configure, and maintain LAN, WAN, and wireless networks
- Monitor network performance and troubleshoot issues related to connectivity, latency, and throughput
- Implement and maintain firewalls, VPNs, and other network security measures
- Configure and manage routers, switches, and other network hardware
- Support integration between on-premises infrastructure and cloud environments
- Apply patches, firmware updates, and security fixes to network equipment
- Conduct network audits and vulnerability assessments
- Coordinate with third-party vendors for equipment maintenance and network expansion
- Respond to network outages and emergencies, including after-hours support when needed
- Document network architecture, configurations, and incident resolutions
- Maintain compliance with IT policies and relevant industry regulations

**Typical decision-making responsibilities:** Choosing network architecture and hardware for a given site or expansion, prioritizing which issues need immediate response during an outage, and deciding how to balance security measures against network performance/accessibility.

---

## 4. Required Skills

### Technical Skills
- Networking fundamentals (TCP/IP, routing, switching, DNS, DHCP)
- LAN/WAN/WLAN design and configuration
- Firewall, VPN, and network security configuration
- Router and switch configuration (e.g., Cisco, Huawei, Mikrotik)
- IP routing protocols (e.g., BGP, OSPF) for ISP/telecom-level roles
- Cloud networking basics (AWS, Azure, or Google Cloud integration)
- Network monitoring and diagnostic tools
- Basic scripting/automation for network tasks (a growing expectation)

### Soft Skills
- Problem solving under pressure (network outages)
- Attention to detail
- Communication and documentation skills
- Teamwork (coordinating with helpdesk, application, and vendor teams)
- Ability to work independently, including in remote or rural locations for field deployments
- Patience and composure during incidents

---

## 5. Work Environment

**Typical workplaces:**
- Telecommunications companies and internet service providers (ISPs)
- Banks and financial institutions
- NGOs and international development organizations
- Government agencies
- General businesses with in-house IT departments

**Work setting:**
- Mix of office-based work and on-site/field work (installing or maintaining equipment at data centers, branch offices, or remote sites)
- Field work is notably more common in this career than in most other IT roles — ISP-based roles may require travel to rural areas for network rollout and maintenance
- On-call availability for outages or emergencies is common, including evenings or weekends
- Laboratory-style data center/server room work is common (equipment racks, hardware configuration)

## 7. Job Outlook

- **Current demand:** Steady, driven by Cambodia's telecommunications sector (fiber-optic and wireless network expansion), banking sector IT infrastructure, and NGOs/international organizations that maintain their own networks across multiple offices or campuses.
- **Future demand:** Expected to continue as internet service providers expand fiber coverage further into provinces and as more organizations depend on always-on connectivity for digital banking, e-commerce, and cloud services.
- **Emerging technologies:** Cloud networking and hybrid on-prem/cloud architectures, network automation and infrastructure-as-code, expansion of fiber-optic and 4G/5G mobile network infrastructure, increased focus on network security amid rising cyber threats.
- **Industry trends:** Growing overlap between traditional network engineering and cloud/security skills, as organizations integrate on-premises networks with cloud services and need to defend against more sophisticated network-based attacks.
- **Growth opportunities:** Cambodian ISPs and telecom operators (fiber and mobile network operators) offer a clear specialization path into large-scale IP backbone network roles; banks and NGOs offer broader infrastructure roles combining networking with general IT support, useful as a stepping stone toward IT management.

## 9. Cambodia Career Context

- **Common employers in Cambodia:** Internet service providers and telecom operators (e.g., EZECOM, SingMeng Telemedia, operating fiber-optic networks covering multiple provinces), banks and financial institutions, and NGOs/international development organizations maintaining multi-site networks (e.g., organizations running programs across Phnom Penh, Sihanoukville, and Siem Reap).
- **Industries hiring this career:** Telecommunications and ISPs, banking and finance, NGO/development sector, general enterprise IT.
- **Government demand:** Present through government agencies maintaining internal IT/network infrastructure, though direct evidence is limited.
- **Private sector demand:** The dominant source of demand, particularly telecom/ISP companies expanding fiber-optic coverage and banks maintaining branch network infrastructure.
- **International organization demand:** Present through NGOs and development organizations operating multiple sites across Cambodia that require dedicated network engineering support.
